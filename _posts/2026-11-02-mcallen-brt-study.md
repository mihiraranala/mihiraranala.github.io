The City of McAllen's Metro transit system is studying the feasibility of a Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) corridor connecting the North Transfer Hub to McAllen Central Station, with a potential extension to McAllen International Airport. BRT is a high-quality, bus-based transit system that runs on shared or dedicated lanes and features amenities such as level boarding and real-time arrival displays. Alongside the transit corridor, the study is evaluating Transit Oriented Development (TOD) opportunities to create walkable neighborhoods with housing, jobs, and services near future stations, taking advantage of Downtown McAllen's high job concentration and existing ridership.

The study was structured in two phases: Phase 1 focused on data analysis, corridor evaluation, and community engagement through surveys and pop-up events, and Phase 2 built on that work to develop final plans, including station locations and TOD strategies.

Mihir served as the GIS and Task Lead for the study, leading the spatial data analysis and corridor evaluation work and managing task-level delivery supporting the BRT and TOD recommendations.
